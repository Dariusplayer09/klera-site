Not another chatbot

Answers are free. Understanding isn't.

You never retype anything

No photographing your notes, no typing an integral on a keyboard with no integral key. You write the maths. That is the input.

The answer lands on your page

Klera writes its working in handwriting, under yours, at the right size, in the right place. One page, not a conversation in another window.

It notices before you say anything

The pause before your first stroke, whether you tried before asking, where your working first went wrong. Measured against your own normal, never shown to you as a score.

Ask for exactly what you need

Five levels of help. You choose how far to go.

  1. Hint

    A nudge toward the idea you are missing. Ask again and it gets more specific rather than repeating itself.

  2. Next step

    One line, the single move that comes next from where you are. Then it stops and waits for you.

  3. Explain

    The why, spoken and on the board. Why this method, why this substitution.

  4. Find my mistake

    It reads your working and points at the line where it went wrong.

  5. Solve

    The whole thing, step by step, at a pace you can follow. A wrong path gets cleared first, so the page ends up correct.

Sometimes it makes you do it

The part that makes Klera feel like a person, not an answer machine.

When Klera can tell you are capable of the next line, it does not write it. It writes one step, explains it, says your turn, and waits.

There is no button. You write the next line and it watches. Right, and it moves on. Wrong, and it tells you something useful without giving the answer away. Properly stuck, and it writes the step itself rather than leaving you staring at a blank page.

Say “just solve it” at any point and it will, from exactly where you got to.

It won't congratulate you for nothing

It keeps track of how much you actually did. Praise you did not earn is worthless, and it makes the praise you did earn worthless too.

It notices things you never told it

Klera reads how the work is going the whole time, not just when you ask.

Bored is not stuck

Everything right, fast, on problems that are too easy? Klera makes the next one harder instead of explaining what you already know.

Struggling well is good

Rubbing something out and fixing it yourself is the best thing that can happen in a session, and it gets left alone.

It marks work you never asked about

Finish something and move on, and it quietly checks whether it was right. Nothing pops up. The next problem is just better aimed.

It knows if you tried first

Solving after one small hint and asking for the whole answer straight away count very differently.

It learns your normal

Some people press harder when they are stuck. Some go completely still before the pen touches the page. It learns which one you are.

You never see a score

No percentage, no streak, no progress bar. They make people work for the number instead of the maths.

Pictures you can pull apart

When a diagram would help, you get a live one.

Klera decides on its own when a diagram is worth drawing, builds it from your actual problem, and puts it on the board beside your working. Drag a slider and everything recomputes, so you watch the idea move instead of being told about it.

If it cannot build something honest for your problem, it says so. It will never invent a graph with made-up numbers.

Sit a real past paper

Built for handwritten STEM exams.

Give Klera a past paper as a PDF. It pulls out the questions and the mark scheme and lays the paper out on the canvas, so you sit it properly, by hand, under time.

Then it marks every answer, teaches the corrections instead of just listing them, and brings the questions you lost marks on back later, pitched just above where you are now.
