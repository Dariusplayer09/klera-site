01 — Why

Built to be needed less.

Every tutoring product is paid by the hour, which means every tutoring product is quietly incentivised to be used more. The better it works, the more you need it.

We wanted to build the opposite thing: a tutor whose job is to make itself unnecessary. That single decision explains most of what is strange about Klera — why four of the five help actions refuse to give you the answer, why tapping Solve is recorded against your independence, and why the system spends so much effort working out whether you are actually stuck or merely thinking.

Thinking looks like doing nothing. Getting that distinction wrong is how a tutor interrupts someone at the exact moment they were about to get it.

02 — Principles

What we decided early and kept.

Silence over a confident guess

When there is not enough evidence, the system reports nothing — not a zero, not a default, not an illustrative example. A zero looks like a measurement. Nothing looks like nothing, which is the truth.

Never invent a student

Anything the tutor is told about a learner must be derived from that learner's recorded work or left out. An early version shipped placeholder text claiming every student "tends to drop negative signs". It reached live prompts. It is the mistake we are most careful about now.

Support is one-directional

Evidence of difficulty can add help. Nothing inferred is ever allowed to take help away. If we are wrong in that direction, a student gets more support than they needed — which is a survivable error.

Measure before steering

When we added a way to tell whether a visualisation helped, we deliberately did not let it change which visualisation gets shown. Change the behaviour first and you lose the baseline that would have told you whether the change worked.

The board belongs to the student

Help arrives as a line of handwriting in the place the line belongs, and only when asked for. No pop-ups over your working, no assistant hovering in a sidebar predicting what you meant.

Write down what failed

Our own engineering notes record the runs that produced nothing and the theories that turned out wrong, because the alternative is paying for the same mistake twice. Some of that is quoted directly on the learner profile page.

03 — Inspirations

What we were looking at.

The one-to-one tutor. The reason a good human tutor works is not that they know more maths. It is that they are watching you, and they intervene at the right moment with the smallest possible nudge. Almost everything in Klera is an attempt to reproduce that specific behaviour rather than the explaining.

Paper. Not a text editor with a maths mode. Not an equation palette. A student's real working is messy, spatial, and crossed out — and all of that mess is signal. So the surface had to be ink, and the tutor had to answer in ink too.

Instruments, not dashboards. A good instrument reports what it measured and is honest about its own uncertainty. We took the idea that a reading below the reliability gate should refuse to display at all from measurement, not from software.

04 — Who

Two of us.

Klera is built by two people. We are also, at the moment, its only two measured students — which is why the learner-profile page can tell you exactly how each of us struggles, and is careful to say that two people is not a study.
