01 — The problem

A past paper you can actually sit.

Past papers are the single most effective revision tool that exists, and almost nobody uses them properly. You print it, you do half of it, you check the answers, you mark yourself generously, and you learn nothing about where you would have lost marks.

Klera takes the PDF, turns it into a paper you sit by hand, and marks it against the real mark scheme.

02 — The flow

PDF in, marked script out.

03 — The grader

The mark scheme wins. Always.

The hardest part of automated marking is not reading the handwriting. It is stopping the model from being clever.

A language model asked to grade will re-derive the answer itself and then mark the student against its own working. When the two disagree, a correct student gets zero. So when a mark scheme is supplied, Klera's grader is instructed in plain terms that the scheme is authoritative by definition: do not re-derive it, do not substitute your own answer, and if your reasoning disagrees with the scheme, the scheme wins.

With no mark scheme, it has to solve the paper first and says so. That is a different and weaker mode, and it is not presented as the same thing.

Marks, not vibes

A number out of the question's real maximum, taken from the paper itself rather than invented.

Where you lost them

Feedback tied to the specific line that cost the mark, not a general comment about showing your working.

The model answer

What the scheme actually wanted — never the grader's own contradicting version.

04 — Corrections

Marked on your own paper.

Feedback you have to mentally re-attach to your working is feedback you will not use. The grader returns correction marks positioned on your script — the way a teacher marks in the margin, against the line that went wrong.

05 — What it feeds

A clean signal, honestly labelled.

An exam question is the least ambiguous data a tutor can have: there is a correct answer, a real maximum, and an official scheme. Whether a student got it right is not an inference.

That makes exam results the most reliable input the learner profile could have. Being straight about it: that connection is not built yet. The grading is real and works today; wiring its results back into the profile is on the list, and until it is done we do not claim it.
