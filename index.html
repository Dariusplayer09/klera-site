Questions?

Here's what students usually ask first.

What is Klera?

Klera is an AI tutor for iPad that works on your handwriting. You write a maths or science problem with Apple Pencil or your finger, and Klera watches how you solve it. When you want help, it gives you exactly as much as you need: a hint, the line where you went wrong, the next step, an explanation or the full solution.

How is this different from ChatGPT?

A chatbot answers what you type. Klera works on the page you're already writing on and follows how you're solving it, not just the question. It points at your actual mistake instead of redoing the whole problem, and it's built to make you do the next step yourself.

When you do want the full working, it writes it on your board by hand and talks you through it.

Will it just give me the answer?

Only if you ask for it. Hint is a nudge, never the answer. Next Step writes one line. Solve is there when you want the full working, but the aim is that you need less help over time, not more.

Do I need an Apple Pencil?

We strongly recommend one. Klera is built around handwriting, and Apple Pencil gives it the precision to read your working accurately and follow each step as you write.

You can still write with your finger, and every feature works, but recognition and the overall experience are noticeably better with a Pencil.

Which subjects does it cover?

Maths and science, from school algebra to university calculus, linear algebra, physics and chemistry. If you can write it on a board, you can ask Klera about it.

Is it free?

Yes. Klera is free to download and every feature is included. There are fair-use limits so the AI stays fast for everyone.

Which devices does it work on?

Klera is made for iPad and needs iPadOS 26 or later. It's on the App Store across Europe.

Is my data private?

Nothing you write is sent to AI until you allow it. You can report any answer, and you can delete your account at any time in Settings. The details are in our privacy policy.

Who is it for?

Students aged 13 and up, from secondary school to university, who want to actually understand what they're solving.
