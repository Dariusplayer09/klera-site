How it works

Three things, every session.

Every session teaches Klera how you learn, so you need less help next time.

Ask for exactly what you need

Six kinds of help. Pick one and watch what Klera does.

A hint lands next to your working

A nudge toward the idea you're missing, placed beside the line you're on. Ask again and it gets more specific instead of repeating itself.

One line, then the pen is yours again

Klera checks the lines you've already written, then writes the single move that comes next, in gold, under your working. Then it stops and waits for you.

Teach me why

Explain gives you the reason, spoken and on the board. Tap Teach me why on it and Klera builds a short lesson from your own problem, with things to drag and questions to answer. Here's a taste, built for the web:

In the app the lesson is written for the problem on your board, and Klera says each line out loud.

It marks the line that went wrong

Klera reads your working, turns into its highlighter and swipes red across the exact line, or the exact term, where it went wrong. Then it tells you why, without solving the rest for you. Spot it first: click the line you think is wrong.

Find the minimum of f(x) = x² − 6x + 5.

  1. 1f′(x) = 2x − 6
  2. 22x − 6 = 0
  3. 3x = −3
  4. 4f(−3) = 32

Line 3. Moving the 6 across makes it +6, so 2x = 6. Try that line again.

Typed here so you can try it. On the iPad the red goes over your own handwriting.

Just the answer, when that's all you want

Checking your result before moving on? Quick Solve puts the answer on the board, typeset as proper maths, in a box you can drag anywhere and pinch to resize.

It's the fastest rung on purpose, and Klera notices when you use it, because asking for the answer straight away and solving after one small hint mean very different things.

The whole thing, one line at a time

Solve works the problem step by step, in handwriting, at a pace you can follow, and talks you through it. If your working went wrong, the wrong path is cleared first, so the page ends up correct.

Sometimes it makes you do it

The part that makes Klera feel like a person, not an answer machine.

When Klera can tell you're capable of the next line, it doesn't write it. It writes one step, explains it, says your turn, and waits.

There's no button. You write the next line and it watches. Right, and it moves on. Wrong, and it tells you something useful without giving the answer away. Properly stuck, and it writes the step itself rather than leaving you staring at a blank page.

It won't congratulate you for nothing

It keeps track of how much you actually did. Praise you didn't earn is worthless, and it makes the praise you did earn worthless too.

It notices things you never told it

Klera reads how the work is going the whole time, not only when you ask.

  • Bored isn't stuck

    Everything right, fast, on problems that are too easy? The next one gets harder instead of explaining what you already know.

  • Struggling well is good

    Rubbing something out and fixing it yourself is the best thing that can happen in a session, and it gets left alone.

  • It checks work you never asked about

    Finish something and move on, and it quietly checks whether it was right. Nothing pops up. The next problem is just better aimed.

  • It knows if you tried first

    Solving after one small hint and asking for the whole answer straight away count very differently.

  • It learns your normal

    Some people press harder when they're stuck. Some go completely still before the pen touches the page. It learns which one you are.

  • You never see a score

    No percentage, no streak, no progress bar. They make people work for the number instead of the maths.

Sit a real past paper

Built for handwritten STEM exams.

Give Klera a past paper or your study material as a PDF. It pulls out the questions and lays the paper out on the canvas, so you sit it properly, by hand, under time.

Then it marks every answer in red pen, right on your handwriting, teaches the corrections instead of just listing them, and brings back the questions you lost marks on.

Also in the app

The rest of what a good tutor does, between problems.

  • Live pictures, when they help

    Klera decides when a diagram is worth drawing and builds it from your problem, in 2D or 3D. Drag it and everything recomputes. If it can't draw something honest, it says so.

  • A study plan by board and topic

    What to revisit next, grouped the way you study. Still no marks out of a hundred.

  • A voice that talks you through it

    Solve and lessons are narrated, so you can watch the page instead of reading a chat.

  • Boards that remember

    Every board keeps its scroll and zoom where you left them, and you can search across all of them.

  • For teachers too

    Classes with join codes, class materials you can mark up with the Pencil, uploaded exams and each student's results.

  • The right paper for the subject

    Blank, graph, geometry or chemistry boards, in light or dark. One theme setting changes the board and every thumbnail with it.
